# Measured results — everything the report needs

Every number here comes from a Kaggle run on real LibriSpeech (via LibriMix), logged in the
notebook outputs. Nothing is simulated or estimated unless a row says so.

Figures are generated by `python report/make_figures.py` into `report/figures/`.

Exact values are transcribed from the runs' own `eval_report.json` (code_version `3f6f1f8`)
and `train_report.json` (`6de9a43` for the frozen-data counter, `ae7fa71` for the final one).

The speaker bootstrap came out *narrower* than Wilson. A cluster bootstrap keeps each drawn
speaker's clips intact, so it measures between-speaker variance and misses the binomial
variation Wilson covers; when speakers are homogeneous it under-covers. **Quote Wilson**, and
report that the speaker bootstrap agreed — that agreement is the finding.

## 5. The dataloader ablation — the headline experiment

One variable changed: whether `set_epoch()` reached the dataloader workers. With
`persistent_workers=True` it does not, so every epoch re-rendered identical mixtures.

| | frozen (one epoch, repeated) | fresh every epoch | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| distinct training mixtures, counter | 128,000 | 2,560,000 | 20× |
| distinct training mixtures, separator | 12,000 | 360,000 | 30× |
| **counter, dev accuracy** | 85.1 % | **90.6 %** | **+5.5 points** |
| counter, train − val gap | +16.2 pp | **−0.8 pp** | overfitting removed |
| counter, best epoch | 8 of 20 | 20 of 20 | still improving |
| counter, final training accuracy | 99.54 % | 89.8 % | memorisation gone |
| **separator, dev SI-SDRi** | +4.51 dB | +4.59 dB | **+0.08 dB (noise)** |
| separator, final train loss | −6.343 | −6.243 | fits training data *worse* |

**The counter was data-limited; the separator was not.** A 0.488 M classifier can memorise
128,000 examples — it reached 99.5 % training accuracy against 83.3 % validation. A 5.25 M
model regressing waveforms cannot memorise 12,000 mixtures, so repeating them cost it nothing.
The separator is limited by optimiser steps (30,000), not by data.

The mitigation is a fixed-length crop divided by its own RMS, which makes level and duration

## 7. Interpretability

**Learned encoder.** 512 filters, 16 samples (2 ms). Peak-frequency quartiles
0 / 1125 / 2359 / 3289 / 4000 Hz; **23 %** peak below 1 kHz, against 47 % for a mel scale and
25 % for a uniform tiling. The basis tiles the band roughly uniformly — it is **not** mel-like.
A peak histogram alone cannot settle whether the encoder is speech-specific; that is left open.

**Mask geometry vs speaker count** (300 test mixtures, 60 per N):

| true N | mask overlap | sparsity | entropy | SI-SDRi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | 0.822 | — | +8.23 |
| 2 | 0.151 | 0.862 | 0.004 | +6.45 |
| 3 | 0.211 | 0.866 | 0.011 | +4.94 |
| 4 | 0.234 | 0.863 | 0.021 | +4.36 |
| 5 | 0.266 | 0.862 | 0.032 | +3.54 |

The prediction was *overlap rises and sparsity falls*. Overlap rises (+0.114). **Sparsity does
not fall** (+0.040) — half the stated mechanism fails, and the report should say so.

**Does geometry predict quality?** Pooling across N is confounded: N moves both variables, and
across the group means alone their correlation is −0.993.

| | pooled r | **within-N r** | per N |
|---|---|---|---|
| mask overlap | −0.668 | **−0.569** | N2 −0.60, N3 −0.55, N4 −0.49, N5 −0.63 |
| mask sparsity | −0.405 | **+0.082** | N1 −0.73, N2 +0.14, N3 +0.25, N4 +0.32, N5 +0.24 |

**Overlap survives the control**; sparsity reverses sign — a textbook Simpson's paradox. So the
mechanistic claim holds for overlap only: among mixtures with the *same* number of talkers, the
ones whose masks collide are the ones separated badly.

**Two independent models.** Counter confidence vs separator SI-SDRi: pooled r = +0.261, but
**within-N r = +0.033**. The architectural independence is real (the two share no parameters),
but the agreement is **not established**.
